What a .com should actually cost

Every domain name carries two unavoidable costs: a wholesale fee charged by the registry that runs the extension (for .com, that is Verisign) and a small fee that goes to ICANN. A registrar's job is to collect those fees, take your details and keep the registration active. Anything on top is the registrar's margin.

Cloudflare Registrar charges only the wholesale and ICANN fees. That is what "at cost" means, and it is why the renewal price equals the first-year price. The registry sets the wholesale price, so it can rise from time to time. Cloudflare's stated policy is to pass on that exact increase and nothing more.

How the $0.99 trap works

None of what follows is illegal, and most of it is disclosed somewhere. The problem is that it is disclosed in small print, after you have already chosen your domain. These are the patterns to watch for:

  1. The teaser price. A first year at $0.99 or $1.99 gets your click. It is often available only for the first term, and only for new customers.
  2. The much higher renewal. In year two the same .com can renew at roughly double the normal price. By then your website and email depend on the domain, so switching feels risky. That is exactly what makes the higher renewal profitable.
  3. Auto-renew on a saved card. The higher price is charged automatically, often with only a short reminder email.
  4. Paid privacy. Some registrars charge around $10 a year to keep your name, address and phone number out of the public WHOIS record. Many have made this free, so check before you pay.
  5. Pre-ticked add-ons. Extras such as email, site builders or "protection" bundles can arrive already ticked in your cart.
  6. Paid extras that are free elsewhere. Email forwarding, SSL certificates and basic security are sold as upgrades by some companies and included for free by others.

When your site runs through Cloudflare, static files such as images, scripts and stylesheets are cached in data centers close to your visitors. Pages load faster and your own server does less work. Caching is included on the free plan, with no per-gigabyte bandwidth charge from Cloudflare.

6. A smaller AWS bill for images

If your images live in an Amazon S3 bucket, AWS charges for data transferred out to the internet, roughly $0.09 per GB after a small free allowance. A cached image is served by Cloudflare, so S3 is only touched when the cache does not have a copy yet.

  • 1,000 GB of image traffic served straight from S3: about $90 a month.
  • The same traffic with 95% of requests answered from Cloudflare's cache: only 50 GB leaves S3, about $4.50 a month.

Your real saving depends on your cache hit rate. To get a high one, point a hostname such as images.yourdomain.com at your bucket through Cloudflare, and set long cache headers (for example Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable) on the files you upload. Set up DNS in about five minutes

  1. Create a free Cloudflare account and open Domain Registration, then Register Domains. Search for your name and check that the price shown matches what you expect, then pay.
  2. Open DNS, then Records. Add an A record with the name @ pointing to your server's IP address. Add a CNAME record with the name www pointing to your domain.
  3. Choose proxied or DNS only. Proxied records (the orange cloud) get caching and protection. Use DNS only for records such as mail servers.
  4. Turn on email forwarding. Open Email, then Email Routing, add an address and the inbox it should forward to, and confirm the verification email. Cloudflare adds the mail records for you.
  5. Set SSL to Full (strict) once your server has a valid certificate, so traffic is encrypted all the way to your server.
  6. Enable DNSSEC from the DNS settings page.

Already own a domain somewhere else?

  1. Add your domain to Cloudflare as a site, copy your existing DNS records and switch the nameservers at your current registrar. Wait until Cloudflare shows the domain as active.
  2. At your current registrar, unlock the domain and request the transfer (authorization) code.
  3. Start the transfer in Cloudflare and enter the code. The transfer adds one year to your registration, charged at the at-cost renewal price.

Under ICANN rules, a domain usually cannot be transferred within 60 days of being registered or transferred. Because your DNS moves first, your site stays online throughout.

What Cloudflare Registrar is not

No option suits everyone. Know these limits before you commit:

  • Your domain must use Cloudflare's nameservers. If you want your DNS elsewhere, this is not the right registrar.
  • Email is forwarding only. If you need a full inbox with sending, add a mail service such as Google Workspace, Proton or Zoho.
  • Not every extension is sold. Check that Cloudflare supports the one you want.
  • The price tracks the registry. At cost works in both directions, so the price rises when the registry's price rises.
  • Support is mostly self-service on a free account, through documentation and the community.

A checklist before you buy any domain

  1. Find the renewal price, not the first-year price.
  2. Multiply it out over five years.
  3. Untick every add-on you did not ask for.
  4. Confirm that WHOIS privacy is free.
  5. Keep auto-renew on and add a calendar reminder a month before expiry, so you never lose the domain by accident.
  6. Turn on two-factor authentication and the registrar lock.
  7. Keep your registration separate from your hosting, so you can move either one freely.

Frequently asked questions

Is Cloudflare really cheaper, or just cheaper in year one?

The price is the same in year one and year ten. The savings come from the renewal price, which is where teaser pricing catches people.

Is it risky to keep my registrar and DNS with the same company?

It concentrates risk in one account, so protect it with two-factor authentication and keep the registrar lock on.

Will my site go down during a transfer?

Not if you move your DNS to Cloudflare first, as described above. The registration transfer itself can take several days, and your site stays online throughout.

Does the at-cost price ever change?

Yes, when the registry or ICANN changes its fees. The change is passed through without a markup.
